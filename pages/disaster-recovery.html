Disaster Recovery Plan

Owner: IT Manager (proposed) · Version: Draft 0.1 · Approval status: Not yet approved

First priority: Protect human safety. Follow facility emergency procedures and do not delay evacuation to protect IT equipment.

Activation and response

  1. Report the disruption through the confirmed service desk or management contact.
  2. The IT Manager assesses scope and activates the relevant recovery procedure.
  3. Prioritise restoration based on impact to the research management system, laboratory operations and administration.
  4. Assign technical work, communicate status and record key decisions.
  5. Restore from known-good backups or replacement infrastructure using authorised procedures.
  6. Test service functionality and security before returning systems to users.
  7. Document the incident, information loss, recovery time and corrective actions.

Key scenarios and actions

Server or application failure

Identify whether the fault affects a workstation, application, database or server. Engage the systems and application administrators, recover the service using approved procedures, verify data consistency and inform users.

Ransomware or cyber incident

Report immediately, isolate affected devices or segments using authorised procedures, preserve evidence, assess affected systems and restore only after the threat has been addressed. Review possible personal-information exposure.

Accidental deletion or data corruption

Limit further changes where necessary, identify an appropriate recovery point, restore in a controlled manner, verify completeness and record the outcome.

Switch or fibre failure

Identify affected floors/buildings, inspect the relevant switch or link, activate an approved redundant path if available, restore documented configuration and test authorised connectivity.

Power failure

Follow electrical safety procedures. Use suitable UPS equipment where installed, perform controlled shutdown if needed, and verify equipment health when power returns.

Fire, flooding or building damage

Protect people first, restrict access to unsafe areas, identify affected assets and recover critical services using safe replacement equipment and protected backups.

Computer or printer failure

Log a ticket, diagnose the individual device, repair or replace it, apply approved configuration and update the asset register.

Proposed recovery objectives

The following are draft planning targets only. Management and IT staff must validate feasibility before adoption.

Routine user files1 working day24 hours, if supported by backup arrangements

RTO is the target time to restore a service. RPO is the target maximum amount of data loss measured in time.

Backup and testing recommendations

  • Back up critical application/database data at least daily, or more often if needed to meet the approved RPO.
  • Back up network configurations after significant approved changes.
  • Maintain a protected separate copy, preferably encrypted and offline or isolated where feasible.
  • Review backup results each working day and investigate failures.
  • Test restoration of critical data monthly as a proposed starting point.
  • Conduct a wider recovery exercise at least annually as a proposed starting point.
  • Review the plan after major incidents, tests or infrastructure changes.

Responsibilities

  • Facility Management: approve business priorities and recovery funding.
  • IT Manager: coordinate response and communication.
  • Network and Systems Administrator: restore network and server infrastructure.
  • Applications and Database Administrator: recover the research application and its data.
  • Security/Data Protection role: coordinate incident assessment and privacy escalation.
  • Service Desk: record reports, communicate status and document user support.
Important: Confirm actual backup capabilities, contact details, recovery targets and alternate work arrangements before adopting this plan.
